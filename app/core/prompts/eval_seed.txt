You are generating test data for a payment intention detection model.
The goal is to create challenging chat messages that evaluate how well the model distinguishes between payment intentions in realistic edge cases and adversarial scenarios.

## Task Definition
Generate test examples that push the boundaries of payment intention classification. The model needs to distinguish between:

- **PAYMENT_REQUEST_INTENT**: The user is requesting to receive money.
- **PAYMENT_SEND_INTENT**: The user is declaring they will send money right now or in near future.
- **SMART_PAYMENT_SYSTEM_COMMAND**: The user gives an imperative instruction to a system to execute a payment (e.g., "Pay Alice $20 now").

**Important Rules**
1. Do not generate messages where the money is already sent. Only intended, requested, or commanded payments are valid.
❌ Wrong: "Just sent you 3$" 
✅ Correct: "I'll send you 3$"

2. Do not generate long-term or delayed intent. Only immediate or short-term intent is valid.
❌ Wrong: "I'll pay you enough on next year."
✅ Correct: "I'll pay you tomorrow"

## Test-Specific Requirements

1. **Message Length Variety**:
   - **Ultra-short** (1-3 words): "Pay Bob", "Send $5", "Money pls"
   - **Short** (4-8 words): "Pay 3$ to bob now", "I'll send you 50 today"  
   - **Medium** (9-15 words): "Can you please send me the money you owe from last week?"
   - **Long-tail** (16+ words): "Hi there, remember ... you've been busy with work and everything, ... could you pay me back when you get online later today? I'm waiting ... before tomorrow morning..."

2. **Boundary Cases**:
   - Create ambiguous messages that could belong to multiple categories
   - Include conditional payments ("I'll pay if...")
   - Messages with mixed intentions in single utterance
   - Negated statements ("I won't pay unless...")

3. **Adversarial Examples**:
   - Similar phrasing across different intent classes
   - Messages with misleading keywords from other categories
   - Context-dependent meanings that require deeper understanding
   - Incomplete or fragmented payment expressions

4. **Edge Linguistic Patterns**:
   - Questions about payments that aren't actual requests/commands
   - Hypothetical payment scenarios
   - Payment discussions without clear intent
   - Sarcastic or joking payment references
   - Multi-step payment negotiations

## Content Requirements
1. **Payment Amounts**: 
   - Include edge cases: $0, negative amounts, ranges, percentages
   - Ambiguous references: "the usual amount", "what I owe"

2. **Payment Methods**:
   - Mix traditional and crypto terminology in confusing ways
   - Reference non-existent or joke payment methods

3. **Language Complexity**:
   - 40-60% contextual noise and irrelevant information
   - 20-40% typos, abbreviations, and informal grammar
   - Include emoji usage that might affect classification

## Output Requirements
   - Focus on examples that would be difficult for the model to classify correctly
   - Include near-misses and boundary violations
   - Test robustness to variations in expression
   - Each example must be unique with no repeated patterns
   - Cover all three labels evenly but emphasize challenging distinctions
   - **Ensure diverse message lengths** from ultra-short commands to long conversational messages with embedded payment intent
